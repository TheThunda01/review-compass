# Review Compass: the project in context

## Background and rationale

A literature review begins with decisions that are easy to leave implicit: what question is being asked, which sources matter, what makes a study eligible, and how the team will document its choices. Review Compass brings those decisions into one small workspace. It is designed for the early conversation between a student or researcher and a mentor, librarian, or review team.

The rationale is transparency, not automatic judgment. [PRISMA-S](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-020-01542-z) describes the information needed to report literature searches clearly. It informs the app's prompts about sources, search strategies, dates, limits, and record management. It does not certify the app or make a filled-in form a complete protocol.

## What it does

The planner helps organize a review question, draft eligibility rules and search strings, plan extraction fields, and export the current work as Markdown. A manual checklist supports search review, while a count calculator checks basic arithmetic in a draft selection flow.

Its keyword view displays literal words and phrases found in a title and abstract. Every record stays **Needs human review**. A negated phrase can still match, and relevant studies may use different terminology. Field counts and mention prompts describe what was entered; they do not score scientific quality or claim that piloting has happened.

## Why it matters

The useful outcome is a draft that another person can question and improve. Showing the evidence behind a software message makes it easier to notice a missing method choice, an overly narrow phrase, or an unsupported assumption. The project also demonstrates a software-design lesson: a simple rule should not be presented as a confident scientific conclusion.

[ASReview LAB v.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2025.101318) illustrates a different, model-assisted screening workflow. Review Compass does not implement that system or inherit its reported performance.

## Key themes

- **Transparent drafting:** make the question and method choices visible.
- **Human review:** keep literal keyword signals separate from eligibility decisions.
- **Reproducible reporting:** preserve enough detail for others to understand the plan.
- **Honest interface language:** distinguish entered text from completed, verified work.
