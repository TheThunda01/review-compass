# Review Compass: the project in context

Terminology: [glossary](glossary.md). For assumptions and their limits, see [assumptions](assumptions.md).

## Background and rationale

Review Compass helps students and researchers make early review decisions explicit: what question to ask, which sources matter, what makes a study eligible, and how to document those choices. It brings these decisions into one workspace for discussion with a mentor, librarian, or review team.

The aim is to make the plan clear enough for others to review. [Rethlefsen et al. (2021): PRISMA-S](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-020-01542-z) describes the information needed to report literature searches clearly. It informs the app's prompts about sources, search strategies, dates, limits, and record management. It does not certify the app or make a filled-in form a complete protocol.

## What it does

The planner helps organize a review question, draft eligibility rules and search strings, plan extraction fields, and export the current work as Markdown. A manual checklist supports search review, while a count calculator checks basic arithmetic in a draft selection flow.

Its keyword view displays literal words and phrases found in a title and abstract. Every record stays **Needs human review**. A negated phrase can still match, and relevant studies may use different terminology. Field counts and mention prompts describe what was entered; they do not score scientific quality or claim that piloting has happened.

## Why it matters

The intended outcome is a draft that another person can question and improve. Showing the text or rule behind a software message helps readers inspect missing method choices, narrow phrases, or unsupported assumptions. The app keeps these prompts separate from scientific conclusions.

[de Bruin et al. (2025): ASReview LAB v.2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2025.101318) illustrates a different, model-assisted screening workflow. Review Compass does not implement that system or inherit its reported performance.

## Key themes

- **Transparent drafting:** make the question and method choices visible.
- **Human review:** keep literal keyword signals separate from eligibility decisions.
- **Reproducible reporting:** preserve enough detail for others to understand the plan.
- **Honest interface language:** distinguish entered text from completed, verified work.
